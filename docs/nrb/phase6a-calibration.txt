THE DECISION THE PLAN ASKS FOR
================================================================================

DOCLING RESCUED PYPDF: 6 of 37 (16.2%).  PYPDF RESCUED DOCLING: 0.

The committed plan says: if this is more than a couple of files, say plainly
that the "pypdf is a fair proxy" claim is NOT established.  Six is more than a
couple.  SO: ON THIS EVIDENCE THE PROXY CLAIM IS NOT ESTABLISHED, and the
profile's `suspicious` count must be read as an UPPER BOUND on unusable
documents, not as a measurement of them.

But every one of the six was read, as the plan requires, and they say something
more specific than "Docling is better".  All six are English STATISTICAL TABLES
or table-heavy notices, and in all six:

  * both engines extracted substantially the same English text;
  * pypdf's legacy_line_ratio lands just above the threshold
    (0.2182, 0.2121, 0.2381, 0.2523, 0.2632 — and one at 0.5787);
  * docling's lands just below it
    (0.1675, 0.1250, 0.1961, 0.1517, 0.1579 — and 0.0214);
  * neither engine found any Devanagari.

That is not Docling rescuing an unreadable document.  It is the SAME false
positive found by hand in Step 5 of the profile: a numeric table whose formatted
numbers ("2,123,180.00") and dash-filled cells trip the intra-word-symbol rule,
tipping a readable English table over 0.20.  Docling escapes it only because its
markdown table rows break lines differently.  Case c807fe4e767e is the clearest:
the "List of Banks and Financial Institutions" table, pypdf 0.5787 vs docling
0.0214 on the same readable English content.

So the correct reading of the six is:

  Docling did NOT rescue a single legacy-font Nepali document — on all 20
  `both_suspicious` files the two engines agree the text is Preeti garbage.
  What the six show is that PYPDF'S TABLE FALSE-POSITIVE RATE IS REAL AND
  MEASURABLE: at least 6 of 37 PDFs (16.2%) in this slice are readable English
  that native-1 called suspicious.

Both statements go in the record.  The screening-proxy claim is not established
by this calibration; the reason it is not established is a classifier weakness
on tables, not a parser weakness in pypdf.  No threshold was tuned, the subset
was not changed, and no favourable example was cherry-picked — all six
disagreements are printed above in full.

SPEED, for the record: 76.2x.  pypdf did the same 37 files in 30.9s that Docling
took 2,354.9s over, and Docling's p95 is 387.8s for a single document.  Whatever
is done about the table false positives, running Docling as the corpus-wide
screen is not the answer to it.
